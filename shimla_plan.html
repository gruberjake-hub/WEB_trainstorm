Landing in Delhi

The hall. Your friend's advice stands: it's a bit of a circus, and the only move is to relax and flow with it. e-Visa holders have their own immigration line. Have three things ready: passport, printed e-Visa, e-Arrival QR on the phone. Then bags, then out to the driver.

Cash. There are bank ATMs in the arrivals hall (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, Axis) — a better rate than the currency counters. If the machine offers to charge you in dollars, say no and choose rupees; the dollar option quietly costs 5–8%. Many machines cap a withdrawal at ₹10,000 (about $120), so take two if you want more. Jake's using his Schwab card for this; your friend's currency-counter route is fine too if that's easier.

Phone. Jake's on Verizon's international day pass ($12/day, only on days it's used) with an eSIM installed as backup. Whatever you choose, turn data roaming on before landing, and expect WhatsApp to be the channel for everything.

Jet lag. An 8 AM landing is nearly ideal going east. The rule for Monday: sleep in the car to Chandigarh all you like, no naps in the hotel bed, stay up until 9 or 10 PM local. Mostly reset by Tuesday.

Colors to avoid

Solid black and solid white are both inauspicious at a Hindu wedding. Dominant red is the bride's. Cream with heavy gold is groom's-family territory — Rohil's father is wearing it.

Shopping in Chandigarh — Tuesday Sept 29

Sector 17 is a walk from the Taj: Manyavar, Tasva, Ethnix by Raymond, and plenty of independents. Ready-to-wear runs roughly ₹10,000–30,000 ($120–360) versus multiples of that at home. Buy Tuesday so Wednesday and Thursday are alteration days; Friday the shops are shut.

Two ways to not do this blind: ask Rohil's family — we're at their house anyway, they're local, and helping foreign guests find wedding clothes is a normal, welcome ask that makes for a warmer first meeting than living-room small talk. Or stick to the branded stores: fixed-price, English-speaking, Western-sized, alterations in-house.

Bring: your measurements, the shoes you'll wear under the outfit (hem length depends on them), and screenshots of the outfit slides from the wedding deck.

If Sunday includes Southall, the Broadway is a preview of all this — it sells the same kurtas and juttis to the UK's Punjabi wedding market. Jake's picking up a cheap backup kurta there as insurance (or in Sector 17 if Sunday goes the Ealing way).

Also buy there: a shawl. Evening events run 7 PM past midnight at 8,250 feet, and a shawl is the correct layer over formalwear. Himachal is famous for them.

Practical notes

Water: bottled only, including for teeth. Hotel restaurants are fine; street ice is not.

Payments: major cards accepted almost everywhere; Apple Pay is not (except on London trains and buses). Keep rupees for small purchases, tips and autorickshaws.

Communication: WhatsApp for everything. Hotel Wi-Fi is good; an international plan covers the gaps.

Power: India uses round-pin plugs (Type C/D), 230 V; the UK uses the big three-prong (Type G). A universal adaptor covers both; phones and laptops don't need a converter. Battery pack for the long ceremony days.

Timing: expect things to start late and run late. The deck says it best: chill vibes.

Altitude and cold: Wildflower Hall is at 8,250 feet. The deck's advice about "modest, loose-fitting clothes" was written by people picturing Delhi. Daytime up there in October is pleasant (60s °F); nights drop into the 40s. Real layers, and a shawl over formalwear for the evenings.

Health: no vaccines required for entry. A small kit — rehydration salts, anti-diarrheal, your usual painkiller, a motion-sickness tablet for the mountain road — and any prescriptions in original packaging.
