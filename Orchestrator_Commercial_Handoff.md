# The Orchestrator — Commercial Handoff

*Carry this into a fresh inference window to open the commercial axis. It is not the archive — the v3 rehydration doc and the project prompt are the archive. This captures only what the design session just produced that bears on pricing, format, and the one-pager, plus the flags that keep the next session honest.*

---

## Where we are

The **experiential spine of the four-hour MVP is built end to end** — Beat 0 (the opening that earns the license) through the denouement. Every seam is load-bearing; every safety property comes from *sequence*, not facilitator vigilance in the moment. That matters commercially for one reason above all: **design is no longer available as the thing to hide behind.** The rehydration doc named pricing, format, and the one-pager as standing avoidance. "The design isn't ready" just stopped being a valid reason to defer them.

The beachhead is decided: **corporate pharma L&D (or adjacent) in a regulated industry.** First beachhead, by design. Other audiences (public, etc.) are explicitly later.

---

## Two non-negotiables that govern commercial choices too

These aren't just design ethics — they constrain pricing and positioning directly.

- **Never withhold.** Every chunk delivers the full value it promises and leaves the participant better than they arrived. Appetite for the next chunk comes from *satisfaction/wholeness*, never a deliberate gap. This is a hard line, paid for once already (the Robbins exit). In commercial terms: the chunking/leveling model is legitimate *only* because each chunk is complete on its own merits. Any pricing or packaging move that ration-gates value to force the next purchase is withholding — name it as such and kill it.
- **Discoverability invariant.** The transformation is auditable: full architecture disclosed before, full account available after; only the *narration* of the transformative moment is withheld in the instant. In commercial terms this is a *procurement asset*, not a constraint — consented and auditable is the opposite of an HR/compliance liability.

---

## Commercial assets this design session generated

These are new inputs the pricing/format/one-pager work now depends on. They did not exist before this session.

- **The value prop is durable — it does not erode as models improve.** The recognition the workshop delivers is *fit, not correctness*: the model's permanent blind spot is the participant's situated world (their audience, review committee, market, what their people actually struggle with). That blind spot doesn't shrink with the next model release — it's structural. **This is the spine of all positioning copy.** A value prop built on "humans still know more" has a shelf life; this one doesn't. Lead with it.

- **The session produces a take-home artifact, not just an experience.** Participants leave with a working personal AI coach they built. And the *Role Codex* (the Stage 1→2 bridge) is now generated as a **byproduct** of the session's own beats — the partner-interrogation produces its declarative layer, the coach-build produces its intent layer. That's visible deliverable value a buyer can point to. Worth pricing against.

- **Structural safety is a pharma selling point.** Consented, auditable, no therapy register, no psychotherapeutic territory — the Integrable Affect / ethics framework is satisfied *by structure*, not by facilitator skill. This is a procurement-friendly story for a regulated buyer who fears "emotional" workshops as liability. Put it in the brief.

- **Facilitator load is high, and the temperament is rare.** Delivery requires someone who can hold a hostile/conscripted room, ally with the skeptic *as* a skeptic, and — hardest — go quiet at the turn and let the room flip itself. The forensics/public-speaking background and mismatcher temperament are load-bearing, not résumé. **This is the central scaling and pricing tension:** founder-delivered = premium, low-volume, high-trust; trainable-facilitator = scalable but requires proving the rails hold for someone who isn't the founder. The design's structural-safety property is the *first evidence* that delegation is possible — but unproven. This tension drives format, price, and margin.

- **Two buyers, two pairs of eyes.** The *participant* transforms; the *buyer* (L&D / CLO) sees an org-wide deployment in what they witnessed. The front-door logic lives in the second pair of eyes. Pricing and the one-pager have to speak to the buyer while the experience serves the participant.

## What has to exist by the end of the next session

Per the rehydration doc, these three still don't exist and **will not fall out of design**:

1. **Pricing architecture** — per-seat, per-cohort, or day-rate; and what the number *signals* about positioning. (Premium/low-volume vs. scalable is the live fork — see facilitator load above.)
2. **Delivery-format options** — in-person vs. virtual vs. hybrid; cohort-size constraints. Note: the room-reading and go-quiet-at-the-turn demands lean heavily in-person for v1.0. Pressure-test whether virtual can hold the seam at all.
3. **The one-pager** — the document you hand the buyer who asks what this is. Built on the durable value prop above.

**Named failure mode for the commercial session:** *pricing-as-avoidance* — the same architecture-as-avoidance tendency in a new costume. Modeling seven elegant pricing structures is not progress. **Success condition: one real number, on one real page, with one real name to send it to.**

## Scope discipline — what is NOT this session's job

Don't let these in; they're real but they're not the MVP-to-market task:

- The full **scale arc** (Stage 2 Applied Practice, Stage 3 Organizational Deployment) — mapped enough; not this session.
- The **arc pinnacle** (full-intensity Dormant Self) — parked, lives deep in the arc with proper runway.
- **Other-audience adaptation** (public, etc.) — explicitly later.
- Further **session-design refinement** — the spine is built. Polishing it is avoidance now.

## Open commercial questions the design surfaced (chew on these)

- Does high facilitator load mean v1.0 is **founder-only** (premium, scarce, high-touch) before it's ever trainable? What does that imply for price and volume?
- What does the **number signal**? In pharma, too-low reads as "another literacy module"; the positioning is closer to executive intervention than training. Price as signal, not just cost-recovery.
- Is the **Role Codex byproduct** priced in, or is it the wedge into the Stage-3 conversation (front door logic)?
- What's the **smallest real first sale** — a single cohort at one pharma company? Who is the actual named buyer, and what's the path to the first booked room?
