Trainstorm exists because most organizations treat AI as a toolset when it is actually an opportunity to redesign how the organization itself thinks. Jake has spent his career at the intersection of learning science, organizational behavior, and emerging technology — and arrived at AI not as a trend to adopt, but as the natural endpoint of everything instructional design has always been trying to do.

His work sits at a level most learning professionals never reach: not building courses, but designing the cognitive infrastructure that makes capability permanent — simulation engines, rehearsal systems, organizational memory architecture, and the governance models that let LD professionals operate as first-class AI stakeholders rather than consumers waiting for IT to provision something.

Trainstorm's methodology was forged in active enterprise engagements, not conceived in a lab. The thinking that enters a client environment originates here. What clients receive is a finished architecture — grounded in real organizational friction, tested against real performance gaps, and designed to outlast the engagement that produced it.
