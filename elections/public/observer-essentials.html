You watch the public parts of the count — and write down what you see.

We're a volunteer team with one guiding principle: a fair and honest election. No party, no formal affiliation, no stake in who wins. Participate as much or as little as you like. This page is the short version of everything that matters. Trust, but verify.

The whole job in one breath

Observe and document. Never interfere.

Your entire role is to see or hear the public parts of the count and make a clean written record. You do not touch materials, direct workers, or take part. A calm, well-documented observer is the most valuable person in the room.

The one hard line
Election observers cannot interfere in the process. You observe; you never obstruct, slow, or disrupt the count or a voter. EL 4.01

Who's who in the room

Know who you talk to — and who you don't.

  • Designated Election Official (DEO) — the person in charge of the location (at central count, usually the board of canvassers or the official they designate). This is the only person you speak to with questions or concerns. Be polite; write down their name. EL 4.02(6)
  • Election inspectors / workers — the people doing the counting and handling ballots. Do not engage them. Route everything through the DEO.
  • You, the observer — a member of the public who has signed in to observe. You are not required to represent any organization, and you do not declare a party. EL 4.02(13), 4.04(1)
Myth: "I have to be a Milwaukee resident to observe at Central Count." False. Any member of the public who signs in may observe — residency is not required.

Getting in the door

Sign in, show ID, get your badge.

  • Tell the DEO you're there to observe. Be friendly — say you're trained and there only to observe and document, not to interrupt.
  • Sign the observer log and show photo ID (it just has to reasonably match your name — it does not need to meet voter-ID standards). Print your name, address, and organization if you have one. EL 4.04(1)
  • Get your observer badge or tag and wear it. You do not declare a party affiliation. EL 4.03(2)(c)
  • Record the time you arrive and the time you leave.

Where you stand & what you may see

Close enough to see — 3 to 8 feet.

Your observation area must be no less than 3 feet and no more than 8 feet from the key tables (where names are announced, where people register, and where ballots are remade). Being able to objectively show that distance is valuable — it's a common point of friction. EL 4.03(1)(b)

You have the right to…
  • Observe all counting at the central count location. EL 4.05(6)
  • Observe rejected certificate envelopes that are set aside. EL 4.03(7)
  • Observe the poll lists, minus confidential portions. EL 4.03(8)
  • Ask the official to repeat a name or address once. EL 4.03(6)
  • One warning before any removal — and any removal must be documented and reported to the WEC. EL 4.03(11)

You may not handle original documents (EL 4.03(9)) or view confidential information (EL 4.03(10)).

What to look for

The main issues, at the count.

These are the situations worth your full attention. If you see one, note it carefully — and find a team leader.

Red flags — central count
  • Absentee ballots received or accepted after 8:00 p.m.
  • Ballot reconstruction/remaking not done by two bi-partisan inspectors — verify the remake number on both ballots.
  • A certificate envelope that's incomplete but counted anyway (see below).
  • Envelope name/address not on the voter list — it should not be counted.
  • Names added to the voter list (this should not happen).
  • Clerk initials missing on an envelope → still counted, but must be recorded on the EL 104. Note it.
  • The time ballots are distributed to the tables by an official.
Certificate envelope — when it's incomplete
  • Voter signature: any mark counts (X, print, scribble). No date required.
  • Witness signature/printed name: signed or printed is fine — either one. Neither → incomplete, cannot be processed.
  • Witness address: needs street number + street name (or "same"/ditto). P.O. Box, city-only, or zip-only → cannot count.

What to log

Every entry, every time.

The record you make is the whole point. Capture only what you saw or heard — actions, not conclusions. For each note:

Capture
  • Time (always) and location — table / ward / station
  • Voter name & number, ward number, when applicable
  • What you saw — the specific action
  • Who — worker/inspector name if you can get it, plus wristband color
  • Witnesses present
  • Was it recorded on the inspectors' EL 104?
  • Any objection you raised, to whom, and their response
  • Evidence and who's holding it

The app does this for you — the Log tab prompts every field, and Submit to team sends it to the shared record. When you're unsure whether something matters, use Check the rules — and when still in doubt, log it.

Recording & devices

At central count, cameras are allowed — with limits.

Central count is not a polling place, so photographing and recording the location is permitted here. (That's different from inside a polling room, where recording is barred until the canvass.) EL 4.05(6)

The limits that still apply
  • Never record passwords or log-ins on tabulators, computers, or machines.
  • Hidden videotaping is illegal in Wisconsin unless you notify the person.
  • Audio recording is OK only if you are part of the conversation.
  • Texting is fine; leave the room to take a phone call.
  • If you can't get a name, a discreet photo is better than nothing.

Decorum & handling people

Calm. Controlled. Constructive. Confident.

Your own emotional state is the single most important factor for doing this well. Most people you meet are fair, professional, and competent. A few won't be — and your job doesn't change either way.

  • Speak only to the DEO — not the workers, not the press, not the voters.
  • Never initiate conversation with a voter. If one speaks to you, respond briefly and point them to an official. EL 4.04(13)
  • No discussing candidates, parties, or ballot questions. No campaign materials or electioneering. Keep your voice low. EL 4.04(9)–(11)
  • Be conversant in the rules and follow both their letter and spirit — precisely, and without heat.
A line that always works
"I'm just here to observe and help have a fair and transparent election."

Focus on the purpose. Lean on your team. Use your resources. You have a right to be there as long as you follow the rules.

If something happens

Document first. Then find a team leader.

  • See a main issue or anything major? Find a team leader — then record everything you can.
  • Note whether the inspectors recorded the incident on their EL 104.
  • Use the app: Check the rules to gut-check it, Add incident to log the facts, Submit to team to send it in.
  • A documented violation may become a formal complaint later — which is why clean, first-person facts matter so much.

Words to know

The vocabulary, briefly.

  • Observe — to see or hear. It does not include handling materials. EL 4.02(12)
  • Observable location — where you're allowed to watch: here, the central count location. EL 4.02(11)
  • Designated Election Official (DEO) — the official in charge you may address. EL 4.02(6)
  • Confidential information — voted ballots and personal voter data you may not view. EL 4.02(5)
  • Electioneering — promoting a candidate, party, or ballot position; prohibited for observers. EL 4.04(9)
  • EL 104 — the inspectors' statement where the counting team records issues (machine issues, ballot additions, remade/late ballots, totals, balance).
  • Certificate envelope — the absentee ballot's outer envelope carrying the voter and witness signatures and address.
