Your to-do list

These are the things only you can do. Two of them (the two travel authorizations) are worth doing this week — they're cheap and quick, and you don't want to be waiting on a government email in late September.

  • Apply for the India e-Visa this week Official site only: indianvisaonline.gov.in (there are look-alike sites that charge triple). Choose e-Tourist. The 30-day version covers our dates; Jake chose the 1-year one because he may stay on a bit. You'll need a passport photo and a scan of the passport photo page; the "reference in India" is Wildflower Hall (address in the lodging section). Government fee for US passports is roughly $25 for the 30-day and $40 for the 1-year — confirm on the site. The government's stated minimum is 4 days before travel, but grants sometimes take longer. When the email arrives, make sure it says GRANTED, not just "received," then print it. You'll need the printout at BA check-in in Chicago and again at immigration in Delhi.
  • Get the UK ETA this week Because we change planes (and terminals) at Heathrow. The official route is the "UK ETA" app or gov.uk/eta; it's £16, usually approved within minutes to a few hours, and lasts two years. Technically there's a temporary exemption for people who stay airside at Heathrow, but our Terminal 3 → Terminal 5 change is the exact case that trips people up, so just get it. Nothing to print — it's linked to your passport electronically.
  • Check your passport now India wants at least six months of validity beyond your arrival date (so valid past March 28, 2027) and two blank pages. If you're close on either, say so this week.
  • Log in to BA "Manage My Booking" this week Reference ZHHJUS plus your last name at ba.com. Confirm all four flights show under your name (the final Heathrow → Chicago leg was cut off on the confirmation we have — please note its flight number and times if you see it), add your passport details, and pick seats. Premium economy on BA is 2-3-2 or 2-4-2 depending on the plane; the pairs by the window are the ones to grab.
  • Tell the wedding side you're coming Friday soon As wedding party, you likely arrive at Wildflower Hall on Friday Oct 2 with the families rather than Saturday with general guests. Whoever is coordinating for Nicole's side can confirm this, and also whether your room is in the wedding block (most likely) or booked separately.
  • File the India e-Arrival card Sept 25–28 A short online form that must be done within 72 hours of landing: indianvisaonline.gov.in/earrival/. Jake will remind you. Keep a screenshot of the confirmation.
  • Money and phone before we go Tell your bank you'll be in the UK and India. Cards work almost everywhere but Apple Pay doesn't, so carry some rupees for small things — an ATM at Delhi airport is the easiest source. Install WhatsApp if you haven't; it's how everyone in India communicates, including the wedding group. Check your carrier's international plan.
  • Pack for a mountain, not for Delhi before we go Wildflower Hall sits at 8,250 feet. Evening events run from 7 PM past midnight outdoors or in open pavilions — bring real layers and a shawl or wrap that works over formalwear (or buy a Himachali one there, they're famous for them). Also: a universal plug adaptor, a battery pack, comfortable shoes for Chandigarh days, and anything you can't buy easily abroad.
Jake has these: confirming the Delhi driver pickup · booking the Oct 5 Chandigarh → Delhi flight · booking the Delhi airport hotel for Oct 5 · the car from Wildflower Hall down to Chandigarh on Oct 5 · his own train ticket up the mountain. You'll get the confirmations as they land.

The route

Getting around

Monday Sept 28 — Delhi to Chandigarh

A private driver, arranged by Rohil's father, meets us at Delhi Terminal 3. It's about 4–5 hours on a good highway. We looked at flying this leg and rejected it — the terminal change and second security line would save under an hour and cost a lot of arrival-day patience. It's also possible the car is shared with other arriving guests; we'll know closer to the date.

Friday Oct 2 — Chandigarh up to Shimla

You ride up with the family convoy — a normal car, about 3½–4 hours of increasingly winding road, arriving at Wildflower Hall around midday. Jake is doing this leg separately on the Kalka–Shimla "toy train," a 5-hour narrow-gauge UNESCO line with 102 tunnels that's been on his list for years; he'll meet everyone at the hotel. The one favor: his bag rides in the convoy with you, since the train coaches are tiny.

Oct 2 is Gandhi Jayanti, a national holiday — shops in Chandigarh are closed, which is why all the shopping happens on the 29th–30th.

Colors to avoid

Solid black and solid white are both inauspicious at a Hindu wedding. Dominant red is the bride's. Cream with heavy gold is groom's-family territory — Rohil's father is wearing it.

Good colors

Jake's plan

A bandhgala or Jodhpuri suit rather than a full sherwani — the bride's father and brother are in sherwanis, and a high-collar suit fits a friend-of-the-party better. It's also wearable again in Chicago, which a sherwani is not. Plus juttis (traditional flat shoes) and a shawl for the evenings. Bringing one cheap kurta set from the US as insurance in case the shopping days go sideways. Headwear is handled — the Pagdi ceremony supplies turbans for all the men.

Shopping in Chandigarh, Sept 29–30

Buying there rather than in Chicago: Sector 17 has Manyavar, Tasva, Ethnix by Raymond and plenty of independents, and ready-to-wear runs roughly ₹10,000–30,000 ($120–360) versus multiples of that at home. Shopping on the 29th and 30th leaves the 1st as an alterations buffer, and the 2nd is a holiday with shops shut.

Two ways to not do this blind: ask Rohil's family — we're at their house anyway, they're local, and helping a foreign guest find wedding clothes is a normal and welcome ask that makes for a warmer first meeting than living-room small talk. Or stick to the branded stores, which are fixed-price, English-speaking, Western-sized, and do alterations in-house.

Bring: your measurements, the shoes you'll wear under the outfit (hem length depends on them), and screenshots of the outfit slides from the wedding deck to show the staff.

Practical notes

Water: bottled only, including for teeth. Hotel restaurants are fine; street ice is not.

Payments: major cards accepted almost everywhere; Apple Pay is not. Keep rupees for small purchases, tips and autorickshaws.

Communication: WhatsApp for everything. Hotel and café Wi-Fi is good; an international phone plan or an airport SIM covers the gaps.

Power: India uses round-pin plugs (Type C/D/M), 230 V. A universal adaptor covers it; phones and laptops don't need a converter. Bring a battery pack for long ceremony days.

Timing: expect things to start late and run late. The deck says it best: chill vibes.

Altitude and cold: Wildflower Hall is at 8,250 feet. The deck's advice about "modest, loose-fitting clothes" was written by people picturing Delhi in the plains. Daytime up there in October is pleasant (60s °F); nights drop into the 40s. Real layers, and a shawl over formalwear for the evening events.

Health: no vaccines are required for entry, but a travel-clinic conversation about routine boosters and a small kit (rehydration salts, anti-diarrheal, your usual painkiller) is sensible. Bring any prescription meds in original packaging.
