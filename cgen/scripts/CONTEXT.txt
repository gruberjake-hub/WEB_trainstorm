What is an Asset Maximization (AMT) This model enables the winning strategy by driving alignment, agility, and measurable value delivery across the asset lifecycle — empowering AMTs to deliver full asset potential and growth.
VISION for maximizing the value of assets & brands
The Asset Maximization Team (AMT) is the end-to-end (E2E) cross-functional team responsible for maximizing the value of an asset across its full lifecycle — from discovery through loss of exclusivity. 

It unites leaders and experts from R&D, Medical, Access, and Commercial into one cohesive and agile team that develops and executes a single, integrated One Asset Strategy.
   Overview
To drive enterprise-level decision-making and delivery, ensuring every function contributes to a unified direction that transforms innovative science into value for patients.
Purpose
Maximize asset value by empowering ONE Team to partner earlier, move faster, and collaborate end-to-end across an asset's life-cycle to form ONE Asset Strategy, and execute that strategy to turn innovative science into VALUE for patients.”

Roles & responsibilities: The role of co-leads 
AMT Co-Leads Operate in Dual Roles Balancing Strategic and Functional Leadership
AMT Co-Leads are accountable for more than their functional expertise. To be effective, they must balance functional responsibilities with above-function strategic leadership. Recognizing and bringing both perspectives to the table ensures strong execution, enterprise alignment, and maximum value creation for each asset.
Functional responsibilities anchor co-leads in their core expertise such as Research, Clinical Development or Brand Leadership; ensuring clinical, regulatory, medical and commercial deliverables are executed with depth, rigor, and accountability throughout the asset lifecycle.
Functional Responsibilities
Above Function Responsibilities
Above function responsibilities elevate Co-Leads into a strategic role, aligning to enterprise priorities & owning the One Asset Strategy. They set long-term asset & brand plans, define objectives and budget, integrate cross-functional perspectives, & remain ultimately accountable for asset success, supported by the AMLT and AMET.

--- Slide 21 ---
Overall budget accountability is given to AMT co-leads for the approved AMT annual budget* i.e. direct cost for research, development, T&M, medical affairs, marketing, access, etc​.
During annual budget process each function is responsible to align on budget with AMT (co-leads) and once approved through budget process* each function is responsible to execute on the budget.
AMT Co Leads & AMLT Team is expected to carefully review the progress of AMT budget and achieve the objective within their approved annual budget.
If necessary, AMT co-leads are empowered to reallocate budget to the most important priority even between VC and VD (e.g. let’s say it is in development vs marketing) The AMT discusses together, and co-leads take the decision on prioritisation and shifting the budget to the most important activity that drives the best impact.
If Budget Transfer is required from VC to VD, or VD to VC, SWIFT Approval is required. 
Step 1: AMT co-Leads to contact their Finance BP Business Partner (FBP) and inform the transfer plan with its background.
Step 2: FBP to contact Enterprise Finance (EF).
Step 3: EF to submit approval request in SWIFT and obtain approval. (quarterly basis & retrospectively).
Step 4: EF to inform the SWIFT decision to FBP (and FBP to AMT co-leads).
AMT co-leads are empowered to flexibly reallocate their budget only within the asset. If additional budget is required, please follow the existing governance process.

To deliver the ONE Asset Strategy, AMTs have clear budget accountability and flexibility within the asset. 
This approach ensures aligned investment decisions and agile resource shifts to maximize strategic impact.

Setting Expectations for Co-Leads and AMLT Members

In this new model, Co-Lead and AMLT roles extend beyond tactical, function-specific responsibilities. These roles require an asset-first mindset and strategic forward thinking—proactively identifying opportunities, anticipating challenges, and driving recommendations rather than waiting for direction. 

While AMLT members continue to bring deep functional expertise, they are now expected to elevate their perspective and represent the overall asset strategy, not just their individual functional priorities. Both Co-Leads and AMLT members should consistently consider “what’s next” and “how to maximize value for the asset”—without relying on task assignments or direction. 

 

What This Means 

AMLT Members 

Translate functional strategies, opportunities, risks, and changes into clear implications for the asset, considering impacts on timelines, budgets, launch strategies, and clinical execution. 

Collaborate across functions to ensure recommendations are informed by broader asset needs and aligned with the One Asset Strategy. 

Co-Leads 

Provide strategic leadership across the asset lifecycle by integrating inputs from AMLT members, extended teams, and stakeholders. 

Ensure cross-functional alignment, facilitate decision-making, and maintain clarity on priorities and trade-offs. 

Act as mentors and enablers to AMLT members, supporting their development into confident, enterprise-minded leaders. 

 

Expectations When Bringing Recommendations 

Recommendations presented to the AMLT should: 

Reflect Collaboration 
Impacts and options have already been discussed and aligned with relevant functions. 

Demonstrate Integration 
The recommendation clearly connects functional insights to the broader asset strategy and enterprise priorities. 

Enable Decision-Making 
Instead of presenting multiple options for the AMLT to choose from, recommendations should propose a well-considered, data-driven path forward, informed by cross-functional inputs and extended team contributions. 

 

The Goal of the Model 

This model empowers Co-Leads and AMLT members to act as strategic leaders, balancing deep functional expertise with an enterprise-wide, asset-focused view. By fostering proactive, integrated leadership, teams can drive asset success more effectively and deliver greater value across the organization. 




Scenario for AMLT Training: Translating Functional Insight into Asset-Level Impact — Global Translational Medicine Lead (EDTS) 

Context: 
The Global Translational Medicine Lead (GTML) is a core member of the Asset Maximization Leadership Team (AMLT), responsible for integrating early clinical and biomarker insights and the translational medicine strategy into the asset strategy.  The translational medicine strategy focuses on informing and supporting clinical development by addressing key knowledge and data gaps identified in Product Quality Framework (PQF) assessments to improve the quality and speed of development. The GTML ensures that translational data not only advances scientific understanding but also informs trial design, dose selection, indication selection, regulatory strategy, and long-term asset differentiation. 

Example Scenario: 
During an EDTS Extended Team meeting, the GTML reviews interim biomarker data from an ongoing Phase 2 trial of Product AST-456, a small-molecule oncology therapy. Results show that a subset of patients with a specific genomic signature (high expression of Gene X) demonstrates markedly better response rates and progression-free survival compared to the overall trial population. 

At the same time, exploratory safety biomarkers suggest that patients with a different profile (low Gene Y expression) may be at higher risk for hepatotoxicity, raising potential safety signal concerns. 



GTML Role in the AMT Meeting: 
The GTML comes to the AMLT prepared not just to present the biomarker findings, but to translate them into cross-functional asset-level implications. They should engage with relevant cross-functional teams beforehand to anticipate potential impacts and inform the discussion or contribute to recommendations — while recognizing that full cross-functional alignment may only be reached during the AMLT meeting, when all stakeholders are present and can weigh in together. See additional details below.  

Clinical Development Strategy: Trial design may need to be adapted to stratify by Gene X expression, or to incorporate biomarker-based enrichment in upcoming Phase 3 studies, with the GTML partnering with Precision Medicine group to execute the gene signature as a diagnostic assay. 

Regulatory Strategy: Regulators may expect companion diagnostic development or enrichment designs to substantiate claims of efficacy in biomarker-defined populations making close partnerships with Regulatory Affairs and Program Management critical. 

Safety Monitoring: Additional risk-mitigation strategies may be needed for patients with low Gene Y expression, requiring closer collaboration with Safety and Clinical Operations. 

Commercial & Medical Affairs: Targeted biomarker strategy may support differentiation versus competitors and inform early payer discussions, while also raising educational needs for clinicians. 

Cross-Functional Dependencies: 

		Clinical Operations: Adjust inclusion/exclusion criteria and site readiness for biomarker testing. 

		Medical Writing, Clinical Operations, Pharmacovigilance:  Engage to update the Investigator’s Brochure (IB) 

		Regulatory: Engage health authorities early on biomarker-driven strategy. 

		Commercial: Revisit patient population assumptions and refine launch forecasts. 

		Diagnostics/Partnerships: Explore feasibility of developing a companion diagnostic with external partners. 

		Intellectual Property (IP) / Legal: secure freedom to operate, protect biomarker and assay innovations, and manage IP. 

Strategic Decision Point: The AMT may need to decide whether to pivot to a biomarker-driven strategy, balancing risks (narrower patient pool, diagnostic development costs) against potential benefits (higher efficacy signal, competitive differentiation, regulatory advantage). 



Why It Matters: 
This scenario highlights the importance of the GTML not just as a generator of scientific data, but as a strategic partner who translates complex biomarker findings into decisions that directly shape asset strategy. By framing the scientific insights in terms of development design, regulatory impact, market differentiation, and cross-functional implications, the GTML enables the AMT to make informed, high-value choices about the future of the asset

Scenario-1 for AMLT Training: Translating Functional Insight into Asset-Level Impact — Research Lead

Context:
The Research Lead (RL) is a core member of the Asset Maximization Leadership Team (AMLT), responsible for bringing forward preclinical and discovery-stage insights that inform the clinical development path, target validation, and long-term differentiation strategy for the asset. The Research Lead is an AMT Co-Lead from Asset Maximization Team (AMT) formation until First-Subject Dosed (FSD) and then transitions to an AMLT member.

Example Scenario:
During a Research Extended Team (RET) meeting, the RL reviews newly generated preclinical data on Product AST-789, a novel immunotherapy currently transitioning from Phase 1 to Phase 2 development. Data from in vivo models show a strong synergistic effect when AST-789 is combined with an existing checkpoint inhibitor. However, the same studies also reveal an unexpected dose-dependent cytokine release profile in non-human primates, suggesting potential safety risks at higher exposures.

Product AST-789 has multiple tumor-type indications under consideration, with high expectations to become a backbone therapy in immuno-oncology. The asset is also under external scrutiny due to competitive programs advancing rapidly with similar mechanisms of action.

Research Lead Role in the AMT Meeting:
The RL comes to the AMLT prepared not only to share data but to frame its asset-level implications. They should engage with relevant cross-functional teams beforehand to anticipate potential impacts and inform the discussion or contribute to recommendations — while recognizing that full cross-functional alignment may only be reached during the AMLT meeting, when all stakeholders are present and can weigh in together. See additional details below. 

Clinical Development Strategy: Should Phase 2 trial designs incorporate the combination strategy earlier to maximize differentiation?

Safety Considerations: Translational safety signals may require tighter dose-escalation and monitoring in clinical protocols, impacting trial timelines and design.

Competitive Landscape: Early synergy data may provide a unique competitive edge if leveraged quickly in trial planning.

Cross-Functional Dependencies:

Development: Protocols would need to be adapted to include combination cohorts or stratified dosing. 

Regulatory: Need to engage regulators early on rationale for combination and dose monitoring.

Commercial: Reassess target product profile (TPP) positioning versus competitors.

Medical Affairs: Prepare external engagement strategy highlighting novel biology and differentiation.

Translational Medicine: determine if the biomarker plan should be modified to monitor cytokine production 

Strategic Decision Point: The AMT may need to decide whether to prioritize combination studies despite added complexity and cost or continue with monotherapy to accelerate timelines and reduce risk. 

Why It Matters:
This scenario illustrates how the Research Lead must bridge early scientific insights with asset strategy. By translating preclinical opportunities and risks into clinical, regulatory, and commercial terms, the RL helps the AMT make informed choices that balance innovation, safety, competitive positioning, and asset value.
